# Game Design Reference

## 1. Purpose

Create a short educational die-and-retry game for Learnex participants who know little about startups and incubation.

The fiction: the player has just joined EDHEC Entrepreneurs with a young startup. They must survive, learn, find traction, and build a viable company over an accelerated incubation journey.

The game should create conversation between participants. A player should naturally be able to say things such as "I survived 14 months", "I became profitable", "I raised a seed round", or "I died because I hired too early."

## 2. Player fantasy

The player should feel like a founder making imperfect decisions under constraints, not like an accountant filling a spreadsheet.

The emotional arc should move from:

- optimism and scarcity;
- first signals and first mistakes;
- increasing stakes;
- hard trade-offs;
- either collapse, viability, fundraising with traction, or an exceptional exit.

## 3. Starting state

Baseline to tune through playtesting:

- Cash: around EUR 10,000
- Founding team: 2 people
- Clients: 0 or very few
- MRR: EUR 0 or low
- PMF: low
- Team state: healthy but fragile
- Product: MVP exists but is imperfect
- Burn: low but non-zero
- Runway: derived from current cash / monthly net burn

The game should not require the player to understand all startup terms before starting.

## 4. Main indicators

Keep four primary indicators maximum on the main HUD.

### Cash

Real euros available. This is the most concrete survival resource.

### MRR

Monthly recurring revenue or an equivalent simplified recurring revenue metric. Display it in euros and explain it briefly the first time.

### Team

A 0-100 style composite for morale, workload, cohesion, and sustainable pace. Avoid splitting this into many separate HR gauges in the MVP.

### Product-Market Fit

A simplified 0-100 indicator of whether the product genuinely solves a valuable problem for a market that wants it.

PMF is pedagogically important because it helps distinguish "I made some revenue" from "I am building something the market repeatedly wants."

### Derived / secondary information

- Burn per month
- Runway in months
- Number of clients
- Current month
- Possibly reputation/credibility later, but not required for MVP

## 5. Time structure

The narrative target is roughly 18 months of incubation.

Do not force 18 long interactive screens if that makes a run exceed 10 minutes. Some months can be short events or automatic transitions. The game may use 10-18 meaningful decision points while still framing the journey as 18 months.

## 6. Three-act progression

### Act 1: Survive and understand the market

Approx. months 1-6.

Themes:

- user interviews;
- MVP iteration;
- first pricing decision;
- first client;
- website/design temptation;
- freelance costs;
- founder focus;
- small events and communities;
- early corporate interest;
- initial burn awareness.

The player should learn that scarce cash and attention matter.

### Act 2: Find traction

Approx. months 6-12.

Themes:

- repeatable sales;
- increasing prices;
- hiring or not hiring;
- corporate POCs;
- focus vs custom work;
- pivot decisions;
- marketing spend;
- grants and non-dilutive funding;
- first investor conversations;
- competition.

The player should learn that revenue, PMF, team capacity, and cash can pull in different directions.

### Act 3: Accelerate without breaking

Approx. months 12-18.

Themes:

- larger recruitment;
- fundraising;
- management strain;
- big corporate contracts;
- international expansion;
- debt or financing;
- competition;
- cofounder tension;
- market changes;
- scaling infrastructure or process;
- strategic acquisition interest.

The player should feel that bigger numbers do not remove trade-offs.

## 7. Failure states

Failure should usually be causal and explainable.

Primary examples:

- Cash <= 0: the startup can no longer operate.
- Team collapses: burnout, founder conflict, or loss of key people makes continuation impossible.
- Extended lack of traction can trigger a softer failure if PMF and revenue remain too low for too long.

Avoid instant random game-over events with no relationship to previous decisions.

## 8. Positive endings

Do not define fundraising as the only win.

### Sustainable / profitable company

Example criterion: revenue covers recurring operating costs for several consecutive months, with acceptable team health.

### Funded with traction

Example criterion: the startup raises a meaningful round while showing enough traction / PMF and ends with significant runway.

Treat this as reaching a major milestone, not as proving the company is permanently successful.

### Exceptional exit

A rare ending. Acquisition interest should require a strong combination of traction, strategic fit, timing, and perhaps prior decisions. Keep it unusual so it feels special.

Potential future endings could include a strong bootstrapped company or strategic partnership, but do not multiply endings before the core loop is good.

## 9. End-of-run recap

The recap is a core feature, not a decorative final screen.

Show:

- months survived;
- ending type;
- final cash;
- MRR;
- client count;
- PMF;
- team state;
- largest burn / current burn if useful;
- 2-4 major decisions that shaped the run;
- principal reason for failure or success;
- one thing the player did well;
- comparison with the previous run or personal best when stored locally;
- immediate retry CTA.

Optionally assign a light player archetype based on behavior, for example:

- The Growth Hacker
- The Bootstrapped Builder
- The Corporate Hunter
- The Product Purist
- The Fundraiser
- The Cautious Founder

Profiles should be playful and descriptive, not moral judgments.

## 10. Die-and-retry loop

The retry loop should be fast:

1. run ends;
2. recap explains the causal story;
3. player sees how far they got and perhaps a best score;
4. one-click retry;
5. some event/order variation makes the next run meaningfully different.

The player should learn through repeated hypotheses:

- "This time I will not hire so early."
- "This time I will focus on customer discovery."
- "This time I will accept the corporate POC."

The next run should challenge that hypothesis rather than simply confirm a fixed optimal route.

## 11. Tone

Use clear, lively French. Keep paragraphs short. Humor is welcome when it helps memorability.

Avoid startup-bro caricature, excessive English jargon, or cynical framing.

Use recognisable ecosystem situations without requiring insider knowledge.

EDHEC Entrepreneurs should feel present as the incubation context through mentors, experts, introductions, workshops, and ecosystem opportunities, but the game should not become institutional advertising.
